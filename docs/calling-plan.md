# Calling plan

Plan and decisions for voice and video calls between partners.

Keep this file current. When a calling decision changes, or calling code lands, update the matching section in the same change. Move items from "Open questions" to "Decisions" once the developer settles them, and replace proposals with what was actually built.

## Decisions

Decided on 2026-11-14.

- The first version has voice and video calls.
- Calling has three entry points, each a phone button. The most important one is in every game toolbar, left of the chat button, so a game toolbar reads score, call, chat, then the "•••" menu (`GameToolbarActions`). The second is in the Us tab's header, beside the streak, top right like the others. The third is in the top bar of the "Just us" couple chat.
- Calls show in the Phone app's Recents as the partner's name with the Paeonia icon (`includesConversationInRecents`). Calling back from Recents or Siri goes through the same start path as the in-app buttons. The system hands back the name the call showed, not an id, because the handle's value is that name (see "System call UI"). A couple has one partner, so a call back to the partner's current name calls the partner. A call back with no partner, or to any other name, such as a name from before a nickname change, is refused with plain copy. Recents keeps the partner's name after sign-out, leaving or account deletion, because the app cannot remove entries from it. The developer accepted that when choosing Recents.
- Drawing together during a call needs no new feature. The live canvas already works whenever both partners have it open, so it works during a call because the call keeps running while people move around the app.
- Outside the app, calls ring on the system call UI with Paeonia's ring (`ringtoneName`), the same ring the app plays inside it. Decided on 2026-11-15, replacing the system ringtone.
- While Paeonia is open on screen, the partner's call rings inside the app instead of the system's banner. A large pill at the top of the screen shows the partner's face and name, "Calling you", and Decline and Answer. Tapping the pill anywhere else opens the full call screen with Answer and Decline, and swiping that screen down while the call still rings goes back to the pill. Answering from either opens the full call screen. The app tells the system about the call only when the person answers, or when the app goes to the background, and then iOS rings as usual. Decided on 2026-11-15, after a test on two iPhones. While the app was active, the live query's ring came about 0.5 seconds before the VoIP push, and iOS did not require a report for the push (`mustReport` false 9 times out of 9). In the background or on a locked phone it did (3 of 3). Reporting the call and asking to join right after the tap took about 20 ms each, and the system activated the call's audio as usual. Declining without ever reporting the call ended it on both phones. Reporting the call as the app left the foreground made iOS ring as usual.
- Every call, voice or video, outgoing or incoming, starts on the built-in speaker. If AirPods, a headset, a car or another external route has the sound when the call's audio starts, the call stays there. The Speaker button moves the sound to the earpiece and back. The proximity sensor is on only while a voice call plays on the earpiece. Decided on 2026-11-15, after a test call on a phone.
- Paeonia has four call sounds of its own, made by `ios/Scripts/game-sounds.swift` in the warm style of the game sounds. While the partner's phone rings, the caller hears a ringback: a soft falling fifth, E6 to A5, once every 3 seconds. It is not a copy of a phone network's ringback or of FaceTime's. It stops at once when the partner answers or the call fails or ends. When a call connects for the first time, both phones play a short rising chime in A (A5, C#6, E6, A6 over a low A4). A reconnect plays nothing. When a call that connected ends, however it ended, both phones play the chime turned around: E6, C#6 and A5 falling, 0.9 seconds long. The partner's phone rings with two quick rising pairs, A5 to E6, once every 2.5 seconds, so it is easy to tell from the falling ringback. The system call UI loops the file as its ringtone. Inside the app, System Sound Services plays it (`CallRinger`), so the ring/silent switch and the person's vibration settings decide whether the phone sounds or vibrates, and the app never touches the call's audio session for it. It stops when the person answers or declines, the caller cancels, the ring runs out, another device answers, or the app hands the call to the system.
- Calls tap like an iPhone's phone calls, with the patterns iOS 27.1 ships in TelephonyUtilities.framework (`telephony_answer_fpo.ahap` and `end_call.ahap`), played with Core Haptics. The caller's phone taps four times quickly when the partner answers, unless the phone is at their ear. A phone taps three times, 0.4 seconds apart, when the partner hangs up or the call drops, but not when this person hangs up. iOS uses the same rules for its own calls (`HapticDescriptorResolver` in `callservicesd`). Haptics play only while Paeonia is open on screen. Decided on 2026-11-15.
- The phone button starts a voice call. Either partner turns on their own camera during the call, so one button fits the game toolbars and nobody's camera turns on without their own tap.
- The call keeps running across the app. Starting or answering a call opens a full call screen. Swiping it down shrinks it to a floating bubble that stays over every screen, games included, until the call ends. A voice call's bubble shows the partner's face, the call time, mute and end. A video call's bubble shows the partner's video. Tapping the bubble opens the full call screen again. The bubble stays clear of the toolbar, the bottom controls and the game chat panel, which report their edges already (`paeoniaBannerBottomEdge()`, `GameChatScreen`).
- Outside the app, the system shows the call on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. During a video call the partner's video continues in system Picture in Picture (`AVPictureInPictureVideoCallViewController`), drawn by an `AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer`, because a Metal view stops drawing in the background. Your own camera keeps sending through `AVCaptureSession.isMultitaskingCameraAccessEnabled`, which needs no entitlement since iOS 18. Inside the app the bubble is used, not system Picture in Picture.
- The call screen shows the partner's local time when it is known. At night their time, from 22:00 to 07:00, the app asks "It's 02:14 for Robin. Call anyway?" before it rings. The hours are the same night that quiet pushes use (`QUIET_NIGHT_START_HOUR` in `backend/convex/lib/apns.ts`). The partner's time zone is hidden while the partner does not share their location (sharing off or paused), because it tells roughly where they are. Without a time zone the app shows no time and does not ask.
- A missed call adds a "Missed call" row with the time and a Call back button to "Just us", and sends a normal push under the Messages switch. It counts toward the app icon badge until the person opens the chat.
- A finished call adds a quiet "Call" row with its length to "Just us". It never marks the chat unread.
- Incoming calls always show the partner's name, as other calling apps do and as the game Live Activity already does whatever the lock screen setting says.
- A person's call media goes only through TURN while they have paused location sharing (sharing on and `paused_until` set). Everyone else connects directly, and uses TURN only when a network blocks a direct connection. A person who pauses sharing to hide where they are stays hidden, because their partner's phone never gets their IP address. A person who never turned sharing on, or turned it off, accepts that their partner's phone may learn their IP address. Location sharing is opt-in, so relaying everyone who does not share would send most calls through Cloudflare's TURN, which Paeonia pays for. Decided on 2026-11-15. Before that, media went only through TURN unless the person shared their location.
- A phone call from the cellular network during a Paeonia call ends the Paeonia call. Paeonia calls do not support hold.
- Voice notes cannot record during a call, and game sounds and other app audio do not touch the audio session while a call runs.

## Call screens

Built on 2026-11-14 in `ios/PaeoniaApp/Features/Calls/` (`docs/implementation-map.md` lists the files).

- The full call screen, the bubble and the ring pill live in a window of their own above the app's window and below the app lock's cover, so they stay over sheets, covers and games. Starting or answering a call opens the full screen. The chevron or a swipe down shrinks it to the bubble, which the person can drag to any corner.
- A call that rings inside the app starts as the ring pill (`CallRingPill`), under the status bar and the Dynamic Island. The window takes touches only on the pill, so the screen under it keeps working. A tap or a pull down on the pill opens the full call screen, which grows out of the pill: the pill's background, the partner's photo and name, and Decline and Answer move to their places on the screen while the rest fades in (`CallMorph.swift`). Going back to the pill runs the same morph the other way. The chevron or a swipe down on the full call screen goes back to the pill while the call rings. A ring that ends unanswered says why in the pill, or on the full call screen if that is open, until the call clears.
- System Picture in Picture starts from the partner's video the person sees. `CallPictureInPicture` keeps every partner video view and picks the one that is in a window, not hidden or transparent, and on screen, whenever the call changes, a view enters or leaves a window, and just before the app goes to the background. A view that is still in a window can be hidden behind the other surface or faded out, and iOS does not start Picture in Picture from it.
- Your own video shows the front camera mirrored and the back camera as it is. Flipping the camera holds the old camera's last frame until the capturer reports the new camera started, and only then changes the mirroring.
- The phone buttons and Call back go through `CallStarter`, which opens the call screen while a call is live and asks the night question first.
- The Us tab's phone button sits in its header row beside the streak, top right like the chat and game buttons. When the status does not fit beside it, the age moves straight under the distance and the status icon and the info button stay centered on both lines.
- An ended call shows why on the call screen until the engine clears the session. A microphone refusal adds an Open Settings button, and any other failure adds the support link.
- "Just us" decodes a message's `call` (`outcome`, `duration_seconds`) and the thread summary's `last_message_call`, which the Just us card and the message banner read. A finished call never marks the chat unread and gets no banner.

## How it works

Every part except the WebRTC library reuses something Paeonia already runs.

- **Media.** WebRTC sends audio and video directly between the two phones, encrypted with DTLS-SRTP, and falls back to TURN when a network blocks the direct path. While a person has paused location sharing, `get_call_ice_servers` returns `relay_only: true` for them, and their phone sends media only through TURN, so the partner's phone never gets their IP address (2026-11-15, see "Decisions"). A couple only ever has two people on a call, so there is no media server. The library is LiveKit's build of Google's WebRTC ([livekit/webrtc-xcframework](https://github.com/livekit/webrtc-xcframework), module `LiveKitWebRTC`, types prefixed `LKRTC`), pinned to an exact release. Paeonia uses only the library, not LiveKit's servers or SDK. It replaced the unmodified build from stasel/WebRTC on 2026-11-15 for its `AVAudioEngine` audio device, which lets the app play its own sounds inside the call's audio and turn down how much voice processing ducks other sound. The relay forwards the session descriptions that carry the encryption fingerprints, so the relay is trusted not to swap them.
- **Compression.** `CallCodecs` sets how a call compresses its media, on both phones. Video uses H.265 first, because every iPhone that runs iOS 27 encodes it in hardware and it needs fewer bits than H.264 for the same picture. LiveKit's default encoder factory leaves H.265 out, so `CallVideoEncoderFactory` adds it in front of the defaults. LiveKit's default decoder factory already decodes H.265. Audio prefers RED-wrapped Opus, which repeats the previous 20 ms frame in each packet, so one lost packet loses no sound. Both phones ask for Opus DTX (`usedtx=1` in the session descriptions), so a phone sends almost nothing while its person is silent. Video sends at most 1.5 Mbps. WebRTC would let 720p reach 2.5 Mbps, and those extra bits cost mobile data and TURN traffic for little gain on a phone screen with H.265. The cap replaces WebRTC's own limit at every resolution, so a picture WebRTC has scaled down, such as 320x180 at the start of a call, may also use up to 1.5 Mbps, where WebRTC would allow 600 kbps. WebRTC's bandwidth estimate still lowers it on a weak connection.
- **Call stats.** While a call is connected, `CallStatsLog` writes one line every 10 seconds to the `CallStats` category of the `no.paeonia.app` log: the audio and video codecs and send bitrates, the video encoder's target, frame size and frame rate, what limits the video, the receive bitrate, lost packets, the round trip time, the estimated outgoing bandwidth, and whether this phone's end of the path is `host`, `srflx`, `prflx` or `relay` (TURN). It logs no addresses. WebRTC's stats name the audio codec Opus even when RED wraps it.
- **Call state.** Convex owns each live call in a `calls` table that holds at most one row per couple, and only while the call rings or runs. Each way a call can end deletes the row and writes the call's row in "Just us". Every state change is a Convex mutation, and both phones follow the row through a subscription.
- **Call setup messages.** The relay (`relay/`) carries only the WebRTC offer, answer and network candidates, after the partner's socket has joined. It gets a `call` channel in `ALLOWED_CHANNELS` (`relay/src/ticket.ts`) and its own ticket mutation, which checks that the person is in the live call. The relay stores nothing, so it never decides whether a call rang, was answered or ended.
- **TURN.** Some networks block a direct connection. For those calls the media goes through Cloudflare's TURN service. A Convex action mints TURN credentials for a member of the live call, with the call id as the identifier, and revokes them when the call ends. Each member gets at most four sets per call. A local backend has no TURN key and returns no TURN servers.
- **Ringing.** `start_call` schedules a one-shot action that sends a VoIP push (`apns-push-type: voip`, topic `no.paeonia.app.voip`, priority 10, expiring at the ring deadline) to every active device of the partner. It has no outbox row and no retries, because a late ring is worse than none. The PushKit token is a `voip_push_token` on the device's `user_devices` row, sent with `register_user_device`, so sign-out and device cleanup already cover it. A ring that APNs calls dead clears only that token, so the phone keeps its other pushes. The payload holds ids and the ring deadline, nothing the person wrote and no lock screen setting. The phone reports the call at once with the partner's name from a small cache that it writes on every unlocked read of the partner, because the local store may be unreadable while the phone is locked. Sign-out clears the cache. A push iOS requires a report for, for a call that already ended, shows the cached partner's name when the caller is that partner, and "Paeonia" otherwise.
- **Ringing while the app is open.** The app also rings from the Convex subscription, and drops a second ring for the same call UUID. That is how two simulators on a local backend, which has no APNs keys, can call each other. While the app is active and iOS does not require a report, `CallEngine.reportIncoming` starts the call without reporting it, keeps the partner's handle on the run (`CallRun.unreportedRingHandle`) and sets `CallSession.ringsInApp`. Answering reports the call to the system and then asks to join, and a refusal fails the call. Declining hangs up without telling the system, which never knew the call. The app hands the ring to the system when it enters the background, and when a push for the same call arrives that iOS requires a report for. It keeps ringing when the app only stops being active, as under Control Center. A system that refuses the report, such as under a Focus, ends the call as missed.
- **Hearing the answer.** Both phones follow the call's row through the Convex subscription, and while the call rings they also read it with a plain `calls:get_live_call` request every 2 seconds (`CallEngine.updateRingWatch`). The subscription's socket can stay on a network the phone has left. In a test on 2026-11-15 the caller had just turned Wi-Fi off, and it rang on for 18 seconds after the partner answered, because ConvexMobile only noticed the dead socket then and has no way to reconnect on request. The calls' other requests open on the current network, so they went through.
- **System call UI.** LiveCommunicationKit's `ConversationManager` shows incoming and ongoing calls on the Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island and in Recents. The app creates it and the `PKPushRegistry` at launch, even when signed out, in a call engine that lives outside SwiftUI views. The handle's value is the partner's name, and its display name is the same. The system call UI shows the value and ignores the display name, which reaches the system only as the Siri name (Apple feedback FB24933340, seen on iPadOS 27.0.1), so a user ID as the value showed a long UUID on the incoming call. Outgoing calls go through `StartConversationAction`. iOS shows its own "Paeonia Audio unavailable" sheet over the app for an outgoing call that the other side ends before it connects, whatever reason the app reports, so on the caller's phone a call the partner declined or did not answer ends with an `EndConversationAction`, as if the caller hung up. A missed incoming call ends as `unanswered`, so Recents shows it as missed.
- **Audio.** WebRTC's audio runs in LiveKit's `AVAudioEngine` audio device, and only inside the audio the system activated (`conversationManager(_:didActivate:)`): `CallAudio` makes the engine available at the activation and unavailable at the deactivation or the call's end. Nothing in the app calls `setActive` during a call. The call's category is set once before the system reports the call, as Apple's DTS asks of call apps: `playAndRecord`, `.default` mode and `.defaultToSpeaker`. During the call the app changes only the speaker option, when the person taps Speaker. In `voiceChat` iOS plays sound that skips voice processing far quieter, which made the ringback barely audible on an iPhone. When the engine's voice processing starts, iOS switches the mode to `voiceChat` on its own, and an output override would not survive that switch, so every call starts on the speaker through `.defaultToSpeaker`, which leaves AirPods, a headset or a car alone. The Speaker button changes that option and the override together. At the activation the engine prepares the microphone without starting, muted at its input mixer, so it starts once, with the voice and the microphone together, when the phones connect. Started for the voice alone, it restarted for the microphone a moment later and cut off the connected chime. Voice processing's own mute crashed the iPhone's audio service (`ATSecureVADManager::setMuteState` in audiomxd, 2026-11-15), so the app never uses it. `CallMachine` asks for the ringback while an outgoing call rings and the system has activated its audio, and for the connected chime on the first connect once audio is active. When a call that connected ends with its audio, the engine stops, the ended sound plays with `AVAudioPlayer` in the call's session, and the system hears that the call ended only after the sound has played. The engine reports the end, or fulfills the system's end request, 0.9 seconds later. The session allows haptics while the microphone records (`setAllowHapticsAndSystemSoundsDuringRecording`), because iOS mutes them otherwise. `CallSoundPlayer` plays a sound inside the engine, on a player connected to the engine's main mixer next to the partner's voice, whenever the engine runs, and otherwise with `AVAudioPlayer` in the call's session. The ringback plays before the engine starts. Voice processing ducks other sound at its lowest level (`duckingLevel = .min`). The sound files are stereo and louder than the game sounds (`docs/game-audio.md`). `CallAudio` logs the session's state at each step, which a device's console shows when the app launches with `OS_ACTIVITY_DT_MODE=YES`. The system activates an outgoing call's audio after the app fulfills the start, as CallKit does, so the ringback starts once `start_call` returns. If it activates later, the ringback starts then. `PaeoniaAudioSession` has a call-active gate that game sounds, the pairing celebration and voice notes check. If `didActivate` has not arrived a few seconds after a join, the call fails instead of running without sound.
- **Access.** Starting a call needs a couple with access (`getCurrentEntitledCoupleId`). A call in progress finishes even if the subscription lapses, so its tickets and TURN credentials check membership in the live call only. `start_call` has a rate limit. Every new public function has a `convex-test` test that someone outside the couple, or without access, is refused.
- **Leaving.** Sign-out, leaving the relationship and account deletion end the live call first, in the existing purge paths.
- **App setup.** The app adds the `audio` and `voip` background modes next to `remote-notification`. `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` and `NSCameraUsageDescription` in `ios/PaeoniaApp/Supporting/Info.plist` mention calls as well as voice messages and photos.

## Backend

Built on 2026-11-14 in `backend/convex/calls.ts`, `lib/calls.ts`, `push:send_call_ring` and the relay's `call` channel. `backend/AGENTS.md` lists the functions and their errors.

- `start_call` takes the call id the app proposes and the phone's device id. A retry with the same id returns the same call, and an id whose call already ended is refused, because the call's row in "Just us" uses the call id as its message id.
- The call's row in "Just us" is a `thread_messages` row with `call: { outcome, duration_seconds }`, the caller as sender and no body. Every message read returns `call` (null for a message), and `get_conversation_threads` returns `last_message_call`. The server refuses to edit, reply to, delete, react to or report it.
- `get_conversation_threads` returns `has_unread` for each chat, decided by the same rule as the app icon's badge (`lib/threadUnread.ts`). It passes over completed calls, so a finished call after an unread message leaves the chat unread.
- The missed-call push is the `missed_call` kind under the Messages switch. It opens `paeonia://chat` and uses the chat's `message` category, so the lock screen says "New message" when previews are hidden. It goes out for a caller cancel or a ring timeout, not for a decline, and not when the person already read the chat.
- A `sweep-calls` cron every 15 seconds ends a running call that lost one side's heartbeat for 60 seconds, and a ringing call whose scheduled timeout was lost.
- Sign-out (`disable_user_device`) ends the person's live call whichever phone signs out. Ending the relationship, which account deletion and bans also do, ends it before the couple ends, so its row still lands in "Just us".
- TURN credentials come from Cloudflare's `generate-ice-servers` endpoint with a 24-hour TTL and the call id as `customIdentifier`. The call row keeps their usernames, and the call's end revokes them. A member who asks a fifth time in one call is refused with `P0003 turn_limit_reached`.

- **Extra rules from the review.** The phone button answers the partner's call when it is ringing. The partner's time zone is cached and refreshed in the background, and the night check waits at most 1.5 seconds for a fresh one. Offers and answers carry an offer number, and an answer to an older offer is dropped. A voice call on the earpiece turns on the proximity sensor. A `start_call` whose answer never arrives sends `end_call`, so the partner stops ringing.

## Call states

| Event | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Start while the partner's call to you rings | `start_call` answers that call instead. Mutations run one at a time, so no tie-break is needed. |
| Start while a call is live for the couple | Refused with "You're already in a call". |
| Answer | The first device to answer wins and is recorded on the row. The person's other devices end their ring as answered elsewhere. |
| Decline | Ends the ring on every device of the person who declined. Writes a missed-call row. |
| Ring timeout | A scheduled mutation ends a call still ringing after 45 seconds and writes a missed-call row. |
| Caller cancels | Ends the ring and writes a missed-call row. No second VoIP push goes out. A late push is reported and ended at once, from the ring deadline and the row. |
| End | Either person ends an active call. Writes a "Call" row with its length. |
| Lost phone | Each phone sends a heartbeat every 15 seconds while the call is active, each one given up after 5 seconds so a slow request never delays the next. A sweep ends a call with no heartbeat from one side for 60 seconds. The other phone ends the call itself after 30 seconds without a connection. |
| Network change | The call shows "Reconnecting", reconnects the relay at once, restarts WebRTC's connection, and ends after 30 seconds without one. |
| Answer before allowing the microphone | The answer goes through at once, because iOS cannot ask for the microphone on a locked screen or in the background. The app asks without waiting, and iOS shows the question when the person opens the app. Until then the call screen says the partner cannot hear them yet. Allowing it starts WebRTC's audio again so it records. Refusing it ends the call as a microphone failure. A microphone the person already refused stops the answer. Starting a call runs in the foreground, so it asks first. |
| Answer from a closed app before first unlock | The join fails and the call ends as failed, because the sign-in token is not readable yet. |

Only a call that is still in the expected state can move. Answer, decline, cancel and timeout each check that the call still rings, so exactly one of them writes the missed-call row.

## Mistakes other teams made

- **A VoIP push that reports no call.** iOS kills the app, and after repeated misses stops delivering its VoIP pushes. Report the call with `reportNewIncomingConversation` before the PushKit handler returns, with the UUID from the push. Fetch anything slow after reporting. A push for a call that already ended still reports it, then ends it at once. ([WWDC26 session 226](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/226/), [Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/817976))
- **No sound after answering.** Audio that starts before the system activates the session gets torn down. Sometimes `didActivate` never fires, which an iOS 18.4.1 thread reports and which still happens on iOS 26. Apple's advice is to never call `setActive` yourself. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/783870))
- **Answering a call while the app is closed.** The join action can arrive before any screen exists, so screens cannot own the call. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/808667))
- **Actions left hanging.** Every `ConversationAction` must end in `fulfill` or `fail`, or the system call UI gets stuck. A failed answer must not go on to join the call. ([Stream's LiveCommunicationKit pull request](https://github.com/GetStream/stream-video-swift/pull/1200))
- **Handles that change.** A handle that is a temporary token breaks calling back from Recents. The usual advice is a stable user ID ([CallKit to LiveCommunicationKit migration](https://www.theswift.dev/posts/migrate-a-calling-app-from-callkit-to-livecommunicationkit/)), but the system call UI shows the handle's value instead of its display name (FB24933340), so Paeonia's handle is the shown name. A couple has one partner, so a call back to that name still finds them. A nickname change leaves old Recents entries that are refused.
- **The side button.** A side button that only locks the screen during a call came from the app's audio handling and route, not the system. Test the side button with the call on the receiver. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/852594))

## Testing

Two simulators on a local backend cover starting, answering, declining and ending a call, the call screen and the bubble. The simulator has no system call UI, so iOS ends every LiveCommunicationKit call there at once. Simulator builds use `SimulatorCallSystem` instead, which hands each request straight back to the engine and activates the audio session itself when the engine fulfills a start or a join. An incoming call rings inside the app there as on a phone. One that arrives while the app is in the background shows nothing, because the simulator has no system call UI. The call sounds play there too, and in the `call-outgoing`, `call-incoming` and `local-flow-call` developer scenarios. The required UI tests, `CallJourneyUITests`, start a call from a game on the `local-flow-call` scenario, shrink it to the bubble, open "Just us" and end it there from the bubble, and on the `local-flow-incoming-call` scenario let the partner call, open the ring pill to the call screen and answer. The simulators get no VoIP pushes, so the developer tests the rest on two real phones: ringing a locked phone, answering from the Lock Screen, a closed app, AirPods and car audio, a network change mid-call, and the side button. The backend tests cover every call state above. `scripts/call-media-probe` checks the compression on a Mac in about 30 seconds. It runs a call between two WebRTC connections in one process, with the app's `CallCodecs.swift` and `CallStatsLog.swift` and the pinned LiveKitWebRTC build. A fake microphone plays a voice-like sound and silence by turns of 1 second, and a fake camera sends panning 720p noise at 30 fps. It checks that both directions send H.265, that audio packets carry two Opus frames while speaking, that silence sends under 30% as many packets as speech, that both session descriptions ask for DTX, that the video encoder's target stays at or under 1.5 Mbps, and that the sent video stays under 1.65 Mbps over every 10 seconds, which allows for the Mac encoder missing its target by up to 10%. With `--defaults` it skips `CallCodecs`, and each of those checks except the last has to fail, which shows the probe can tell them apart. It skips the sent video check there, because without the cap WebRTC sets its own limit from the resolution it picks, so the result changes from run to run. It uses the Mac's VideoToolbox encoders, not the phone's, and that encoder can send up to about 15% over its target in a 2-second stretch, mostly when it changes resolution.

## Open questions

- Whether score, call, chat and "•••" fit in every game toolbar on the smallest supported iPhone. Check each game with `scripts/scenario-screenshot` once the button exists.

# Calling plan

Decisions and external constraints for voice and video calls between partners.

Keep this file current. When a calling decision changes, update the matching section in the same change.

## Decisions

- The first version has voice and video calls.
- Calling has three entry points, each a phone button. The most important one is in every game toolbar, left of the chat button, so a game toolbar reads score, call, chat, then the "•••" menu (`GameToolbarActions`). The second is in the Us tab's header, beside the streak, top right like the others. The third is in the top bar of the "Just us" couple chat.
- Calls show in the Phone app's Recents as the partner's name with the Paeonia icon (`includesConversationInRecents`). Calling back from Recents or Siri goes through the same start path as the in-app buttons. The system hands back the name the call showed, not an id, because the handle's value is that name (see "System call UI"). A couple has one partner, so a call back to the partner's current name calls the partner. A call back with no partner, or to any other name, such as a name from before a nickname change, is refused with plain copy. Recents keeps the partner's name after sign-out, leaving or account deletion, because the app cannot remove entries from it. The developer accepted that when choosing Recents.
- Drawing together during a call needs no new feature. The live canvas already works whenever both partners have it open, so it works during a call because the call keeps running while people move around the app.
- Outside the app, calls ring on the system call UI with Paeonia's ring (`ringtoneName`), the same ring the app plays inside it. Decided on 2026-11-15, replacing the system ringtone.
- While Paeonia is open on screen, the partner's call rings inside the app instead of the system's banner. A large pill at the top of the screen shows the partner's face and name, "Calling you", and Decline and Answer. Tapping the pill anywhere else opens the full call screen with Answer and Decline, and swiping that screen down while the call still rings goes back to the pill. Answering from either opens the full call screen. The app tells the system about the call only when the person answers, or when the app goes to the background, and then iOS rings as usual. Decided on 2026-11-15, after a test on two iPhones. While the app was active, the live query's ring came about 0.5 seconds before the VoIP push, and iOS did not require a report for the push (`mustReport` false 9 times out of 9). In the background or on a locked phone it did (3 of 3). Reporting the call and asking to join right after the tap took about 20 ms each, and the system activated the call's audio as usual. Declining without ever reporting the call ended it on both phones. Reporting the call as the app left the foreground made iOS ring as usual.
- Every call, voice or video, outgoing or incoming, starts on the built-in speaker. If AirPods, a headset, a car or another external route has the sound when the call's audio starts, the call stays there. The Speaker button moves the sound to the earpiece and back. The proximity sensor is on only while a voice call plays on the earpiece. Decided on 2026-11-15, after a test call on a phone.
- Paeonia has four call sounds of its own, in the warm style of the game sounds (see [the audio standard](game-audio.md)): a ringback the caller hears while the partner's phone rings, a rising chime when a call first connects, the same chime turned around when a connected call ends, and a ring for the partner's phone. The ringback is deliberately not a copy of a phone network's or FaceTime's, and the ring sounds different from it so the two are not confused. Inside the app, System Sound Services plays the ring (`CallRinger`), so the ring/silent switch and the person's vibration settings decide whether the phone sounds or vibrates, and the app never touches the call's audio session for it.
- Calls tap like an iPhone's phone calls, using the patterns iOS ships in TelephonyUtilities.framework, played with Core Haptics. The caller's phone taps when the partner answers (unless it is at their ear), and a phone taps three times when the partner hangs up or the call drops, but not when this person hangs up. Haptics play only while Paeonia is open on screen.
- The phone button starts a voice call. Either partner turns on their own camera during the call, so one button fits the game toolbars and nobody's camera turns on without their own tap.
- The call keeps running across the app. Starting or answering a call opens a full call screen. Swiping it down shrinks it to a floating bubble that stays over every screen, games included, until the call ends. A voice call's bubble shows the partner's face, the call time, mute and end. A video call's bubble shows the partner's whole video, in the shape their phone sends it (`CallSession.partnerVideoAspectRatio`), and a pinch makes it bigger (up to 2.5 times) or a little smaller (down to 0.75 times). Tapping the bubble opens the full call screen again. The bubble starts above the bottom controls, such as the tab bar or a chat's composer, which report their edges (`paeoniaBannerBottomEdge()`). The person can drag it anywhere from under the toolbar to the bottom of the screen, over those controls too, and it stays where the finger lets go, without sliding on. A pinch moves the bubble with the fingers while it resizes. Pushed over the left or right edge, a quarter of it and at most 24 pt, or flung hard at it, it tucks away as the same tab the partner's avatar uses, and a tap or a pull on the tab brings it back. Only the keyboard and the open game chat panel (`GameChatScreen`) push it up (2026-11-15, after the developer could not put it at the bottom of a game or tuck it away). While the partner shares their screen, the shared screen gets a window of its own over the app, in the shape of their screen (`partnerScreenAspectRatio`). It first shows right above the bubble, or right below it when there is no room, or else against the other edge, so the two never start on top of each other. It moves, pinches and tucks away on its own, with its own tab, and a tap opens the full call screen with the shared screen as the big video. A tap on the bubble opens it with the partner's camera filling it. A voice call shows the screen's window beside the voice bubble (2026-11-16, the developer's design).
- During a video call the full call screen hides its controls, the partner's name and the dark edges after 4 seconds without a touch, so nothing covers the partner's video. The previews stay and move into the room the controls leave, except under Reduce Motion. The partner's news and the call's own notices above the buttons move down into that room too, to the bottom of the screen, and glide back up above the buttons when a tap brings them back, with the same exception. A tap anywhere that isn't a button brings them back, and a tap while they show hides them at once, as FaceTime does. They never hide while the call connects or reconnects, or while the app waits for the microphone. For someone who uses VoiceOver, Switch Control or the Extra controls setting they hide only over the partner's shared screen, and only by the person's own action (2026-11-16). While the partner's shared screen is the big video they never hide by themselves, as the decision on the fitted shared screen below says.
- On the full call screen one of up to three videos fills the screen: the partner's camera, the partner's shared screen, or the person's own camera. The others are previews in one corner, stacked from the corner toward the middle, so they never cover each other. They shrink to fit between the chevron's row and the controls, to no less than 0.6 times. A tap on a preview makes it the big one, and the big one takes the preview's place, so it is easy to go round all three. When the partner starts sharing, their screen takes the big spot, because that is why they shared. When a video goes away, the next one in the person's order fills the screen, and the change animates. The person's own camera fills the screen only while another video can swap back with it. Every video keeps its one view through a swap, so a paused partner's last frame stays on screen. Each preview takes the shape of its video. The person's own camera takes the shape of the frames it sends (`CallSession.localVideoAspectRatio`), so the viewfinder shows exactly what the partner sees. It used a fixed 104 by 152 point tile before, which cut the top and bottom off the camera's 9:16 picture (2026-11-16, after the developer asked why the viewfinder did not match the video). Each call starts over with the shared screen first, then the partner's camera, then the person's own (2026-11-16, the developer's design). The big shared screen does not fill and crop like a camera. It shows whole, in its own shape, beside the previews or over the whole safe area (see the next decisions), and its corners round like a phone's screen at that size: 14% of its width, the share an iPhone's screen corners take of its width (55 pt on a 393 pt phone, 62 pt on a 440 pt one). iOS has no public value for the radius, and the partner's phone could be any model (2026-11-16, the developer's request).
- The person drags the previews together to any of the four corners, as in FaceTime. They follow the finger and settle in the corner the throw heads for, under the chevron's row or above the controls, and move into the room the controls leave while they are hidden over a camera. The corner stays for the rest of the app's run, through the bubble and the next call. Before this the camera was fixed at the top right, and a drag on it shrank the call (2026-11-16, after the developer could not move it).
- While the partner's shared screen is the big video, the full call screen has two states. While the controls show, the screen keeps its shape and fits in the room that the top bar, the controls and the previews leave, so nothing covers it. The previews stay in the person's corner. They stack down its side, or sit side by side along its top or bottom edge, whichever lets the screen show larger, so an upright phone's screen sits beside them and a screen turned on its side sits above or below them. They shrink, to no less than 0.6 times, when that lets the screen grow. Dragging them to another corner moves the screen to the side they left. A tap anywhere hides the controls and the previews, and the screen fills the safe area. Another tap brings them back. The controls never hide by themselves over the shared screen, because the screen would then grow without being asked, and they come back whenever the shared screen becomes the big video. VoiceOver, Switch Control and the Extra controls setting reach the same two states through the shared screen, which is a button for them. The videos move between the states, and under Reduce Motion they fade out and back in instead. Until its first picture the screen takes an upright phone's shape. The dark edges under the top bar and the controls do not show over the shared screen. While the controls show, none of the screen is under them, and while it fills the display the controls are hidden. Decided on 2026-11-17, after the faces and the controls covered what the partner wanted to show, and the developer asked for a fitted state and a tap for the full one. Before this the shared screen always filled the safe area, with the previews and the controls over it.
- While a call is under way, the full call screen has a message button in its top right corner, as Message sits on the partner's avatar. It shrinks the call to its bubble and slides "Just us" in from the right over the app, the same as a tap on the partner's message notice (`CallOverlayModel.openCallout`). The call's window never holds a chat, so a call that ends leaves the chat where it is (2026-11-16, the developer's decision). This replaces a panel that raised "Just us" from the bottom of the call's window, under the call shrunk to a preview. That panel needed a second copy of the chat in the call's window, which does not get the app's environment, and "Just us" then arrived two ways.
- Share your screen sits left of the message button, so the row at the bottom keeps room for five buttons on the smallest phone. It is a plain button that asks the call engine to show the system's picker (`startScreenShare()`), and sharing starts only once the person picks there. While the person shares, the button is white and a tap stops it, and "You're sharing your screen" with Stop shows above the controls and beside the bubble, because the system's red sign is easy to miss in the Dynamic Island (2026-11-16).
- The share always says what it is doing. `CallSession.screenShare` is `off`, `starting`, `on` or `failed`. Once the person picks in the system's picker, the share button shows a spinner and takes no tap until the stream runs. The picker can close without telling the app, so the time it is up shows nothing. When the picker cannot show or the stream does not start, "Sharing didn't start. Try again." shows where the call's other notices show, for 4 seconds or until the next tap on Share. While the bubble is tucked away during a share, its tab carries the share button's symbol in red and VoiceOver hears "You're sharing your screen" with its name, because the Stop that hangs off the bubble is gone with it. A tap on the tab brings the bubble back with Stop. The tab's dot counts the share's failure, the camera-off offer and the weak connection as news waiting, as it counts the partner's callout (2026-11-16, after a review found a failed start said nothing and a tucked bubble hid the share).
- One partner shares at a time, because each phone would otherwise show the other's screen inside its own. A share started while the partner shares takes over, and the partner's share stops by itself, as in FaceTime. The system's picker is the step that keeps a stray tap on Share from ending the partner's share. While the partner shares, VoiceOver says that starting a share stops theirs. Two phones cannot tell which share is newer from the order their messages arrive, because shares started inside the relay's delay each arrive after the other phone's own. So each share takes a turn number one higher than any turn either phone has used in the call, and the phone whose share has the lower turn stops it (`CallShareTurns`, in `CallMediaSession`). Equal turns mean both started before either heard the other, and then the caller's share stays. A phone gives way the moment it hears of a share that wins, so when that share had already stopped before its start arrived, the phone's own share stops for nothing and the person starts it again. That takes both partners starting and one stopping inside the relay's delay, a fraction of a second, so no round of confirmations guards against it. A share that is still starting when the partner's starts has no turn yet, so it keeps going and takes over once it runs. Decided on 2026-11-17, after the developer asked for FaceTime's behavior. It replaces the rule of 2026-11-16, under which the share button was dimmed while the partner shared and the caller's share stayed when both started.
- Sharing the screen shows everything on it, the private nickname for the partner and anything else in Paeonia included, as holding the phone up to the camera would. Paeonia hides nothing while a person shares. The system's picker says the whole screen is shared, and a name the app swapped while sharing would have to change in every screen, widget and cache that holds it (decided 2026-11-16).
- The app stays upright on an iPhone. During video on the full call screen, a phone held on its side turns the call like the Camera app: the big video turns to fill the screen the way the phone is held, the previews move to the corner that matches the person's corner as they see it, and the symbols on the buttons turn in place. The buttons' names fade out while the phone is on its side, because a turned name has no room in the row. The call follows `UIDevice` orientation, which still reports while the screen stays upright and stops while Portrait Orientation Lock is on, which is right. Face up, face down and upside down keep the last turn. Under Reduce Motion the videos fade out and back in turned. A voice call's face and name and the top bar's text stay upright. An iPad turns its own screen and only the difference counts. The bubble and the screen's window already take the shape of the partner's picture (2026-11-16, the lead's decision after the developer asked for Picture in Picture to turn).
- The call says what goes wrong in plain words above the controls and beside the bubble, where the partner's news shows, and not over the middle of the video: "Robin's connection is weak" or "Your connection is weak", and with the camera on, an offer to turn it off while the battery is low or the phone is hot, with Turn off and Not now (`dismissCameraOffSuggestion()`). A partner video that stops blurs its last frame, with a spinner while new frames are late, and with a pause sign and "Robin's camera is paused" while their phone paused the camera, on the full call screen and in the bubble. The partner's shared screen shows the moment their phone says it started, before its first picture, so until then it is a dim tile with a spinner, and when its pictures stop it blurs with a spinner as the camera does (`CallSession.isPartnerScreenStalled`), on the full call screen and in its window over the app. The blur is a material over the video, because the video is a UIKit view that a SwiftUI blur does not reach (2026-11-16).
- During a video call with the back camera on, a zoom slider sits above the call screen's buttons and hides with them. It goes from 0.5× to 10×, counted as the Camera app counts it, and starts at 1× each time the back camera starts. The back camera is the phone's combined back camera (`CallCamera`), which switches lenses as it zooms, so 0.5× uses the ultra wide where the phone has one. `CallCamera` finds that camera itself, because LiveKit's `captureDevices()` lists only the single wide lenses, and with the plain 1× lens the slider stopped at 1× (2026-11-16, after the developer could not reach 0.5× in a call). The front camera has no slider (2026-11-16).
- Either partner can share their whole screen during a call, other apps included, and the partner watches it live. Sharing starts only from Paeonia's share button, which shows the system's screen sharing picker (`SCContentSharingPicker`), and begins once the person picks there. ScreenCaptureKit captures the screen inside the app (`CallScreenCapture`), with no broadcast extension. The share keeps sending while Paeonia is in the background, because the app declares the `screen-capture` background mode, and it stops when the person taps stop, when they stop it from the system's indicator, and when the call ends. SharePlay is not used, because the developer does not want it. Decided on 2026-11-16. The first plan was a ReplayKit Broadcast Upload Extension started from `RPSystemBroadcastPickerView`. The iOS 27 SDK deprecates that picker ("Use ScreenCaptureKit SCContentSharingPicker instead"), Apple's ScreenCaptureKit documentation says "A broadcast extension is no longer necessary", and iOS 27 is Paeonia's minimum. LiveKit's Swift SDK made the same move in September 2026 ([pull request 1135](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/pull/1135)). The switch removes the extension's 50 MB memory limit, the socket that carried frames from the extension to the app, and an extension that could outlive the call. ScreenCaptureKit needs no entitlement on iOS ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/856067)). App Review guideline 2.5.4 allows a background mode only for its purpose, so the review notes should say that `screen-capture` sends the person's screen to their partner during a call, after they choose it in the system's picker.
- The shared screen is a third media section in the first offer, next to audio and the camera, so starting or stopping a share swaps a track and never needs a new offer. A `screen` relay message tells the partner's phone when it starts and stops, as `camera` does, and carries the running share's turn. The screen keeps its own shape at no more pixels than 1280x720, which is 652 by 1412 on a phone whose screen is 1179 by 2556, and sends up to 30 frames a second on either path. When bits or the encoder run short, WebRTC sends a smaller picture and keeps the frame rate (`maintainFramerate`), because softer text is easier to watch than a picture that jumps. The screen's video source is not in WebRTC's screen-sharing mode, which caps the encoder's quantizer and drops frames instead (see "Mistakes other teams made"). The screen may use 2.5 Mbps of a direct call's 4 Mbps and 1 Mbps of TURN's 1.5 Mbps, and gets four times the camera's share (`bitratePriority`) of a network too slow for both. While the camera sends too, the camera keeps the rest, 1.5 Mbps direct and 0.5 Mbps on TURN, so the TURN bill stays where it was. The partner shows the screen large and the camera small, so while this phone shares, its camera captures the format nearest 640x480's pixel count in the camera's own shape, at 15 frames a second, and goes back to 1080p at 30 when the share ends. A flip, or turning the camera off and on, keeps the small format during the share. When a share starts or ends while iOS has the camera stopped, such as when the person ends it from another app, the camera starts again in its new size once iOS lets it run. ScreenCaptureKit sends up to 60 frames a second on iOS, where its frame interval cannot be set, so `CallScreenCapture` hands WebRTC at most the screen's limit, and the newest frame wins. A hot phone lowers the screen too, as the decision on heat below says. `scripts/call-media-probe` checks these limits on a screen with a video playing on it. There the screen keeps 30 frames a second on both paths and shrinks its picture to fit the bits, where WebRTC's screen-sharing mode sent 10 frames a second direct and 4 on TURN. A still screen sends no new frames, so the last one goes again every second. Sharing moves the sound from the earpiece to the speaker, as turning on the camera does, and the proximity sensor stays off while either partner shares. Decided on 2026-11-16, after the developer saw a share between two iPhone 18 Pro Max phones arrive as stop motion, at about 1 to 5 frames a second, with the sharing phone warm, where FaceTime's share between the same phones was smooth and cool. The first version captured 1080p's pixel count in screen-sharing mode, sent at most 15 frames a second, and sent fewer frames when bits ran short.
- While a person shares their screen, their bubble and Picture in Picture keep showing the partner's live video, because people expect to see each other while they share. iOS captures the whole screen, the app's own windows included, and has no way to leave a window out ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/842905)). So the partner sees their own video in the shared screen, and the shared screen changes every frame even while the person only reads, which costs bits and power. Showing the partner's photo instead would have let a still screen cost almost nothing to send, and the developer turned that down (2026-11-16).
- While both partners have Paeonia open, the partner's avatar floats in the same window (`Features/Presence/`). The ring pill grows out of it where it sits, with the photo on the avatar's side, and the call screen grows out of it through the shared photo. The bubble takes its spot, so a call starts where the partner already was on screen and nothing travels to the top and back. While the avatar is away, or the keyboard is up, the ring pill shows at the top. A missed call says so on the ring pill and adds to the avatar's unread count, with no banner after it (2026-11-15, after the developer found the extra toast and the trip to the top noisy). Call on the avatar's toolbar closes the sheets and covers over the app first, because the night question is an alert in the app's window under them. Decided on 2026-11-15. The call says what the avatar would, because the avatar is under it (2026-11-15, after the developer lost track of the partner during a video call). The bubble says which game or drawing the partner just opened, under it or above it near the bottom of the screen. The full call screen says that and the partner's messages and notifications above its buttons while the call is under way, because it covers the app's banners. It also says when a game newly waits for this person, because it covers the game. It reads the same waits as the badges and the game's Live Activity. A finished board game waits for nobody on the server, so the call says separately that a board game ended and who won. This is the game's news, not the avatar's, so it shows while either partner hides their presence, and only on the full call screen. A tap shrinks the call to its bubble and opens the game, the drawing or the chat (2026-11-16).
- Outside the app, the system shows the call on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. During a video call the partner's video continues in system Picture in Picture (`AVPictureInPictureVideoCallViewController`), drawn by an `AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer`, because a Metal view stops drawing in the background. Your own camera keeps sending through `AVCaptureSession.isMultitaskingCameraAccessEnabled`, which needs no entitlement since iOS 18. Inside the app the bubble is used, not system Picture in Picture. iOS can leave Picture in Picture on screen when the person comes back, next to the bubble, so the app stops it each time it becomes active. When the call ends or the partner's camera turns off, the app releases its `AVPictureInPictureController`, because `stopPictureInPicture()` does nothing while the app is in the background and would leave a black window on screen. Picture in Picture starts from the partner video the person sees, which changes when the call screen and the bubble swap. Each new source view gets a new controller, because AVKit keeps watching the first view a controller had and reports a source set later through `contentSource` as never visible. While the partner shares their screen, Picture in Picture shows their screen instead of their camera, because the screen is what a person wants to keep watching in another app. The window's content switches between the two and its source view stays, so a running Picture in Picture goes on (2026-11-16).
- The call screen shows the partner's local time when it is known. At night their time, from 22:00 to 07:00, the app asks "It's 02:14 for Robin. Call anyway?" before it rings. The hours are the same night that quiet pushes use (`QUIET_NIGHT_START_HOUR` in `backend/convex/lib/apns.ts`). The partner's time zone is hidden while the partner does not share their location (sharing off or paused), because it tells roughly where they are. Without a time zone the app shows no time and does not ask.
- A missed call adds a "Missed call" row with the time and a Call back button to "Just us", and sends a normal push under the Messages switch. It counts toward the app icon badge until the person opens the chat.
- A finished call adds a quiet "Call" row with its length to "Just us". It never marks the chat unread.
- Incoming calls always show the partner's name, as other calling apps do and as the game Live Activity already does whatever the lock screen setting says.
- A person's call media goes only through TURN while they have paused location sharing (sharing on and `paused_until` set). Everyone else connects directly, and uses TURN only when a network blocks a direct connection. A person who pauses sharing to hide where they are stays hidden, because their partner's phone never gets their IP address. A person who never turned sharing on, or turned it off, accepts that their partner's phone may learn their IP address. Location sharing is opt-in, so relaying everyone who does not share would send most calls through Cloudflare's TURN, which Paeonia pays for. Decided on 2026-11-15. Before that, media went only through TURN unless the person shared their location. Since calls connect while they ring, the partner's ringing devices get the caller's address before anyone answers, and a ringing device sends its own only once its person answers on it. The developer accepted that on 2026-11-16.
- A call connects while it rings, so the two people hear each other about as soon as someone answers. Both phones start the call's media when the call starts ringing: the caller's once `start_call` returns, and each of the partner's devices when its ring arrives, by VoIP push or the live query. Each fetches the network servers, opens the peer connection and joins the call's relay room, and the caller sends its offer. A ringing device takes the newest offer and gathers its own candidates ahead (`iceCandidatePoolSize`), but creates no answer and sends nothing until its person answers on it and the server accepts that answer (`CallMediaSession.answer()`). So no sound or picture leaves a phone before its person answers, and only the device that answered connects when the partner has an iPhone and an iPad. A decline, a ring that runs out, an answer on another device or a cancel closes the session. A start that fails while the call rings tries again at the answer, and a start that fails after the answer ends the call. Each phone logs the milliseconds from the answer to the first connection under the `CallStats` category, and sends them in its quality report. Before this the media started only after the answer, so the answering phone first waited for the network servers (up to 5 seconds when Cloudflare is slow), the relay ticket and the caller's offer. Decided on 2026-11-16.
- Every call sends a quality report, whatever the person chose about usage statistics. When a call ends on a phone, the phone sends `calls:report_call_quality` once, without delaying the end, and drops a send that fails. The report says how the call ended, whether the phones connected and how many milliseconds after the answer, the path the media last took (direct or TURN), the reconnects and the seconds they took, the median round trip, the share of the partner's audio and video packets this phone lost, the seconds of video it sent and received, the hottest thermal state, and the app build. It holds no content, no location and no device id. The server keeps the first report per call and person, refuses anyone outside the call's active couple, deletes a couple's reports when the relationship ends, and deletes every report after 30 days (`retention.ts`). "Download my data" includes the person's own reports. A device whose call was answered on another device sends none, because the answering device reports for the person. `scripts/call-reports` prints the newest reports, and `--hosted` reads api.paeonia.no. The privacy policy describes the report. Decided by the developer on 2026-11-16, so call quality can be read without the dashboard. Since 2026-11-16 the report also says where a shared screen's frames go, because two iPhone 18 Pro Max phones showed a share at 1 to 5 frames a second and the sharing phone got warm, while FaceTime's share stayed smooth, and Console on the phones is hard to read. A phone that shared adds the seconds it sent, the frames per second at each step (ScreenCaptureKit's pictures counted in `CallScreenFrames`, the encoder's input from the screen's `media-source`, and the sent rate), the sent size, the encode milliseconds and average quantizer per frame, key frames per minute, the encoder's name and whether it is power efficient, the share of the sending time each `qualityLimitationReason` held (from `qualityLimitationDurations`, or from the samples when WebRTC leaves it out), and the pixel format of ScreenCaptureKit's buffers. A phone that received a screen adds its seconds, frames per second, dropped frames, freezes and decode milliseconds per frame, from the screen's own inbound stream, which the stats find by its mid. The partner's screen still counts in the video loss and the seconds of video received. Every report adds the camera's sent frames per second and its limit shares, because the camera sends beside the screen, and how often the encoders logged a reset, a failed frame or a dropped frame. Those counts come from WebRTC's own log through `LKRTCCallbackLogger`, matched on the VideoToolbox encoders' lines in webrtc-sdk m150. The H.265 encoder logs its resets and dropped frames as info, so the logger takes info lines while the call's stats run. Frame rates are medians of the 2-second samples. The `CallStats` line every 10 seconds shows the same numbers.
- A direct call sends video up to 1080p at 4 Mbps. A call through TURN sends at most 720p at 1.5 Mbps, so the TURN bill stays where it was. The camera captures 1080p, or less beside a shared screen (see the decision on screen sharing), and `CallCodecs.limitVideo` sets the limits from `CallVideoLimits`, the one place for the camera's and the shared screen's limits on each path and at each heat. Each phone reads the path from the call's stats when the phones connect and every 2 seconds after, and either end on TURN counts as TURN. Reading it again catches a new path after a network change, and a partner's TURN candidate that first showed as `prflx` because it answered before its description arrived. WebRTC lowers the size and rate further when the network estimate is lower. A test call on 2026-11-15 between an iPhone on 5G and an iPad on Wi-Fi went direct over IPv6 (`path host`). The iPad sent 720p at the old 1.5 Mbps cap with 2.7 Mbps estimated free. The iPhone's 5G upload was estimated at 300 to 700 kbps with no lost packets, so it sent 270p to 540p, which no cap changes. A speed test on that phone measured 6 Mbps upload, so a test build forced at least 1 Mbps of video on direct calls. The round trip grew from 70 ms to 1.4 seconds, the partner's phone asked for 95 to 296 packets again every 10 seconds, the video fell to 1 or 2 frames a second and the call kept dropping. WebRTC's estimate was right and the speed test does not show what a call can use, so the app sets no minimum video bitrate. Decided on 2026-11-15.
- A hot phone sends less video. At the thermal state `serious`, or in Low Power Mode, the video sends at most 720p at 24 frames a second and 1.5 Mbps. At `critical` it sends at most 540p at 15 frames a second and 600 kbps. The camera captures at the same lower frame rate, because capturing heats the phone too. The video takes the lower of the path's and the heat's limit on each, and goes back up when the phone cools. iOS lowers FaceTime's frame rate at `serious`, and Apple asks apps to stop using the camera at `critical`. The limits are ceilings, so WebRTC still goes lower on its own when the network or the encoder needs it (`qualityLimitationReason` `bandwidth` or `cpu`), and the two never pull against each other. A shared screen goes smaller before it goes slower as the phone heats up: at most two thirds of its size at 24 frames a second and 1 Mbps when warm, and half its size at 15 frames a second and 400 kbps when hot. That is a third and an eighth of the pixels each second, the same steps the camera takes. The camera beside it keeps the rest of the heat's limit, 500 kbps when warm and 200 kbps when hot, so the two together never pass the camera's own limit (2026-11-16, replacing 10 and 5 frames a second at full size after the developer saw a share arrive as stop motion).
- At `critical`, while the person's camera is on, the call offers to turn the camera off (`CallSession.cameraOffSuggestion` `.hot`). It offers the same with the battery at 20% or less and the phone unplugged (`.lowBattery`), where iOS itself warns about the battery. Heat comes first when both apply. The offer goes away when the camera turns off, the phone cools below `critical`, or it is plugged in. Once the person dismisses one, that reason does not come back during the call (2026-11-16).
- The call says when the connection is weak, and whose end it is (`CallSession.weakConnection`). Each phone judges what it sends from the stats it reads every 2 seconds, and tells the partner's phone over the relay (the `connection` message), because the partner's own sending estimate is the clearest sign of a slow upload that loses nothing. A sample is bad when, over the last 4 seconds, the partner missed 10% or more of the packets, the round trip took half a second or more, or WebRTC left the video under 150 kbps because of the network. The loss counts every packet the phone sends, the shared screen's included, and the video is the camera's, or the shared screen's while it sends without the camera. It is good under 4% loss, under 0.3 seconds and with 250 kbps or more. Three bad samples in a row, about 6 seconds, turn the warning on, so a blip of a second or two never shows, and five good ones in a row, about 10 seconds, turn it off, so it does not flicker. Fewer than 40 packets in the window say nothing about loss, which covers two silent people whose audio sends almost nothing. A phone cannot tell its own upload from the partner's download, so a phone that sends badly names its own end, and when both send badly, each phone names its own. A dropped connection clears the warning, because the call shows "Reconnecting" (`CallConnectionHealth`, 2026-11-16).
- When the partner's camera is on but no frame has come for a moment, the screens blur the last frame instead of holding it still (`CallSession.isPartnerVideoStalled`). The moment is 1 second, or 3 times the usual gap between the last 30 frames, up to 3 seconds, so video that runs at 1 or 2 frames a second on a weak connection is not blurred between frames. It counts from the first frame after the camera turns on (`CallFrameGaps`, 2026-11-16). The partner's shared screen is watched the same way while they share, because their phone sends the last picture again every second while the screen is still, so only a stream that stopped counts.
- When iOS stops a person's camera for now, the partner sees it as paused, not off (`CallCameraState.paused`, the `camera` message's `paused` state). That covers leaving the app without Picture in Picture, another app taking the camera, multitasking and pressure on the camera, so every reason in `AVCaptureSession.wasInterruptedNotification` counts. When the interruption ends, the camera is `on` again. A paused camera keeps its video window, so the partner's Picture in Picture stays open and shows the last frame blurred under a pause symbol, instead of a frozen last frame or their photo (`CallPictureInPicture.setCameraPaused`). This settles the open question about the camera iOS stops in the background (2026-11-16, after the developer asked for a pause symbol).
- A phone call from the cellular network during a Paeonia call ends the Paeonia call. Paeonia calls do not support hold.
- Voice notes cannot record during a call, and no other app audio touches the audio session while a call runs. Game sounds play during a call inside the call's own audio engine, next to the partner's voice, at their normal levels. They follow the speaker, the earpiece or AirPods. The echo canceller should treat them as the call's own playback, so the partner should not hear them back. Two simulators showed them playing in the engine on 2026-11-16, and a call between two phones still has to confirm the partner hears none of them. They stay silent while a call rings. Decided on 2026-11-16, after the developer could not hear game sounds during a call.

## Design notes

- **Ringing.** `start_call` schedules a one-shot action that sends a VoIP push (`apns-push-type: voip`, topic `no.paeonia.app.voip`, priority 10, expiring at the ring deadline) to every active device of the partner. It has no outbox row and no retries, because a late ring is worse than none. The PushKit token is a `voip_push_token` on the device's `user_devices` row, sent with `register_user_device`, so sign-out and device cleanup already cover it. A ring that APNs calls dead clears only that token, so the phone keeps its other pushes. The payload holds ids and the ring deadline, nothing the person wrote and no lock screen setting. The phone reports the call at once with the partner's name from a small cache that it writes on every unlocked read of the partner, because the local store may be unreadable while the phone is locked. Sign-out clears the cache. A push iOS requires a report for, for a call that already ended, shows the cached partner's name when the caller is that partner, and "Paeonia" otherwise.
- **Ringing while the app is open.** The app also rings from the Convex subscription, and drops a second ring for the same call UUID. That is how two simulators on a local backend, which has no APNs keys, can call each other. While the app is active and iOS does not require a report, `CallEngine.reportIncoming` starts the call without reporting it, keeps the partner's handle on the run (`CallRun.unreportedRingHandle`) and sets `CallSession.ringsInApp`. Answering reports the call to the system and then asks to join, and a refusal fails the call. Declining hangs up without telling the system, which never knew the call. The app hands the ring to the system when it enters the background, and when a push for the same call arrives that iOS requires a report for. It keeps ringing when the app only stops being active, as under Control Center. A system that refuses the report, such as under a Focus, ends the call as missed.
- **Hearing the answer.** Both phones follow the call's row through the Convex subscription, and while the call rings they also read it with a plain `calls:get_live_call` request every 2 seconds (`CallEngine.updateRingWatch`). The subscription's socket can stay on a network the phone has left. In a test on 2026-11-15 the caller had just turned Wi-Fi off, and it rang on for 18 seconds after the partner answered, because ConvexMobile only noticed the dead socket then and has no way to reconnect on request. The calls' other requests open on the current network, so they went through. The caller also takes the phones connecting while the call rings as the answer, because a ringing device connects only after its person answered.
- **System call UI.** LiveCommunicationKit's `ConversationManager` shows incoming and ongoing calls on the Lock Screen, in the Dynamic Island and in Recents. The app creates it and the `PKPushRegistry` at launch, even when signed out, in a call engine that lives outside SwiftUI views. The handle's value is the partner's name, and its display name is the same. The system call UI shows the value and ignores the display name, which reaches the system only as the Siri name (Apple feedback FB24933340, seen on iPadOS 27.0.1), so a user ID as the value showed a long UUID on the incoming call. Outgoing calls go through `StartConversationAction`. iOS shows its own "Paeonia Audio unavailable" sheet over the app for an outgoing call that the other side ends before it connects, whatever reason the app reports, so on the caller's phone a call the partner declined or did not answer ends with an `EndConversationAction`, as if the caller hung up. A missed incoming call ends as `unanswered`, so Recents shows it as missed.
- **Audio.** WebRTC's audio runs in LiveKit's `AVAudioEngine` audio device, and only inside the audio the system activated (`conversationManager(_:didActivate:)`): `CallAudio` makes the engine available at the activation and unavailable at the deactivation or the call's end. Nothing in the app calls `setActive` during a call, except WebRTC after an interruption ends or the audio service restarts (see "Mistakes other teams made"). The call's category is set once before the system reports the call, as Apple's DTS asks of call apps: `playAndRecord`, `.default` mode and `.defaultToSpeaker`. During the call the app changes only the speaker option, when the person taps Speaker. In `voiceChat` iOS plays sound that skips voice processing far quieter, which made the ringback barely audible on an iPhone. When the engine's voice processing starts, iOS switches the mode to `voiceChat` on its own, and an output override would not survive that switch, so every call starts on the speaker through `.defaultToSpeaker`, which leaves AirPods, a headset or a car alone. The Speaker button changes that option and the override together. At the activation the engine prepares the microphone without starting, muted at its input mixer, so it starts once, with the voice and the microphone together, when the phones connect. Started for the voice alone, it restarted for the microphone a moment later and cut off the connected chime. Voice processing's own mute crashed the iPhone's audio service (`ATSecureVADManager::setMuteState` in audiomxd, 2026-11-15), so the app never uses it. `CallMachine` asks for the ringback while an outgoing call rings and the system has activated its audio, and for the connected chime on the first connect once audio is active. When a call that connected ends with its audio, the engine stops, the ended sound plays with `AVAudioPlayer` in the call's session, and the system hears that the call ended only after the sound has played. The engine reports the end, or fulfills the system's end request, 0.9 seconds later. The session allows haptics while the microphone records (`setAllowHapticsAndSystemSoundsDuringRecording`), because iOS mutes them otherwise. `CallSoundPlayer` plays a sound inside the engine, on a player connected to the engine's main mixer next to the partner's voice, whenever the engine runs, and otherwise with `AVAudioPlayer` in the call's session. The ringback plays before the engine starts. Voice processing ducks other sound at its lowest level (`duckingLevel = .min`). The sound files are stereo and louder than the game sounds (`docs/game-audio.md`). `CallAudio` logs the session's state at each step, which a device's console shows when the app launches with `OS_ACTIVITY_DT_MODE=YES`. The system activates an outgoing call's audio after the app fulfills the start, as CallKit does, so the ringback starts once `start_call` returns. If it activates later, the ringback starts then. `PaeoniaAudioSession` has a call-active gate that the pairing celebration and voice notes check. `CallSoundPlayer`'s engine delegate methods also hand the engine to `GameSoundPlayer`, which moves its players into it and connects them to the main mixer. Game sounds play there while the engine runs and stay silent while a call has no running engine, such as while it rings. They never start or stop the engine. If `didActivate` has not arrived a few seconds after a join, the call fails instead of running without sound.

## Call states

| Event | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Start while the partner's call to you rings | `start_call` answers that call instead. Mutations run one at a time, so no tie-break is needed. |
| Start while a call is live for the couple | Refused with "You're already in a call". |
| Answer | The first device to answer wins and is recorded on the row. The person's other devices end their ring as answered elsewhere. A device sends its answer to the caller's offer only after the server accepted its answer. |
| Several ringing devices | Each joins the call's relay room when its ring arrives, and the caller sends a new offer with the next number for each one that joins. A ringing device keeps only the newest offer. An answer to an older offer is dropped, and the answering device then answers the newer one, so the call ends in one connection. A device that joins after the answer, before the phones connect, costs one more offer and a restart of the network checks. |
| Decline | Ends the ring on every device of the person who declined. Writes a missed-call row. |
| Ring timeout | A scheduled mutation ends a call still ringing after 45 seconds and writes a missed-call row. |
| Caller cancels | Ends the ring and writes a missed-call row. No second VoIP push goes out. A late push is reported and ended at once, from the ring deadline and the row. |
| End | Either person ends an active call. Writes a "Call" row with its length. |
| Lost phone | Each phone sends a heartbeat every 15 seconds while the call is active, each one given up after 5 seconds so a slow request never delays the next. A sweep ends a call with no heartbeat from one side for 60 seconds. The other phone ends the call itself after 30 seconds without a connection. |
| Network change | The call shows "Reconnecting", reconnects the relay at once, restarts WebRTC's connection, and ends after 30 seconds without one. |
| Audio interruption | When something else takes the sound for a moment, such as an alarm or Siri, WebRTC stops its engine and starts it again when the interruption ends. |
| Audio service restart | When iOS restarts its audio service, the app gives the session the call's category again, replaces its audio engine and sound players, and the call goes on. If the audio is not back after 5 seconds, the call ends as failed instead of running without sound. |
| App closed during a call | When the person swipes the app away, `applicationWillTerminate` hangs up and waits up to 4 seconds for the server to hear it, so the partner's call ends at once. iOS does not call it for a crash or an out-of-memory kill, and those end like a lost phone. |
| Answer before allowing the microphone | The answer goes through at once, because iOS cannot ask for the microphone on a locked screen or in the background. The app asks without waiting, and iOS shows the question when the person opens the app. Until then the call screen says the partner cannot hear them yet. Allowing it starts WebRTC's audio again so it records. Refusing it ends the call as a microphone failure. A microphone the person already refused stops the answer. Starting a call runs in the foreground, so it asks first. |
| Answer from a closed app before first unlock | The join fails and the call ends as failed, because the sign-in token is not readable yet. |

Only a call that is still in the expected state can move. Answer, decline, cancel and timeout each check that the call still rings, so exactly one of them writes the missed-call row.

## Mistakes other teams made

- **A VoIP push that reports no call.** iOS kills the app, and after repeated misses stops delivering its VoIP pushes. Report the call with `reportNewIncomingConversation`, with the UUID from the push, and call PushKit's completion handler only after the report returns. Apple says to report before the delegate method returns, and a `Task` that reported later crashed apps on the older delegate methods. The report is `async`, so it cannot finish before a synchronous delegate method returns. The iOS 27.1 PushKit runs its check (`_terminateAppIfThereAreUnhandledVoIPPushes`) inside the completion handler it passes to the delegate, not after the delegate method returns. That was read from the disassembly of `-[PKPushRegistry voipPayloadReceived:mustPostCall:withCompletionHandler:]` in the iOS 27.1 simulator runtime on 2026-11-15, so check it again when a new iOS changes PushKit. Fetch anything slow after reporting. A push for a call that already ended still reports it, then ends it at once. Send VoIP pushes only for a ring, never for a cancel, because every push needs a report. ([WWDC26 session 226](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/226/), [Apple forums on the report rule](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/774958), [Apple forums on `mustReport`](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/816211), [Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/817976))
- **No sound after answering.** Audio that starts before the system activates the session gets torn down. Sometimes `didActivate` never fires, which an iOS 18.4.1 thread reports and which still happens on iOS 26. Apple's advice is to never call `setActive` yourself. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/783870))
- **Answering a call while the app is closed.** The join action can arrive before any screen exists, so screens cannot own the call. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/808667))
- **Actions left hanging.** Every `ConversationAction` must end in `fulfill` or `fail`, or the system call UI gets stuck. A failed answer must not go on to join the call. ([Stream's LiveCommunicationKit pull request](https://github.com/GetStream/stream-video-swift/pull/1200))
- **Handles that change.** A handle that is a temporary token breaks calling back from Recents. The usual advice is a stable user ID ([CallKit to LiveCommunicationKit migration](https://www.theswift.dev/posts/migrate-a-calling-app-from-callkit-to-livecommunicationkit/)), but the system call UI shows the handle's value instead of its display name (FB24933340), so Paeonia's handle is the shown name. A couple has one partner, so a call back to that name still finds them. A nickname change leaves old Recents entries that are refused.
- **A WebRTC helper that turns the call's audio off.** WebRTC's audio engine reacts to interruptions through `LKRTCAudioSession`. After an interruption ends, or the audio service restarts, that object sets the session active or inactive by its own count of activations, which stays at zero unless the app reports the system's activation with `audioSessionDidActivate`. At zero it deactivates the call's session in the middle of a call. Two simulators in a call showed it on 2026-11-15: after a simulated interruption the phone sent 0 kbps of audio for 20 seconds, and with the activation reported it kept sending. `CallAudio.noteSystemActivation` reports it. This makes WebRTC call `setActive(true)` after those two events, which is the one exception to "nothing in the app calls `setActive` during a call". The library also keeps its dead `AVAudioEngine` after a restart of the audio service, so `CallAudio.rebuildAfterServiceRestart` releases and replaces it. ([WebRTC's `RTCAudioSession.mm`](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/m150_release/sdk/objc/components/audio/RTCAudioSession.mm), [Apple Q&A QA1749](https://developer.apple.com/library/ios/qa/qa1749/_index.html))
- **A call that depends on TURN's API.** Most calls connect directly, so an app that fails the call when its TURN provider's credential request fails turns a small outage into no calls at all. When Cloudflare refuses, fails or takes longer than 5 seconds, `get_call_ice_servers` returns Cloudflare's public STUN server alone. Only a person who must relay, because they paused location sharing, is refused then, since a direct connection would give away their address. ([Cloudflare's TURN documentation](https://developers.cloudflare.com/realtime/turn/generate-credentials/))
- **Several devices that answer while it rings.** Signal connects every device of the person called while the call rings, which needed ICE forking, their change to WebRTC that gives each device its own connection over one shared set of candidates. One plain peer connection can answer only one device ([Signal's blog](https://signal.org/blog/ice-forking/)). WebRTC also keeps a single DTLS ClientHello that arrives before the answer, a later one replacing it, and replays it when the answer comes ([WebRTC's `dtls_transport.cc`](https://chromium.googlesource.com/external/webrtc/+/cd430c84d95e36b37ae00981e89324defd8bed0e/p2p/base/dtls_transport.cc)). Two ringing devices that both set their answer could leave the caller replaying the other device's handshake. Paeonia's ringing devices only take the offer and gather candidates, and set their answer when their person answers.
- **Packet loss that goes negative.** `packetsLost` counts from the sequence numbers, and a packet that is resent and arrives twice can make it negative ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/RTCInboundRtpStreamStats/packetsLost)). The quality report counts a negative value as no loss.
- **A weak connection judged from one sample.** Common detectors flag a network issue from a single stats interval, such as more than 5% loss or 200 ms of jitter between two reads ([webrtc-issue-detector](https://github.com/VLprojects/webrtc-issue-detector/blob/master/src/detectors/OutboundNetworkIssueDetector.ts)), which shows a warning for a two-second blip. LiveKit leaves jitter and round trip time out of its connection quality and rates loss and bitrate ([LiveKit](https://kb.livekit.io/articles/2455399507-how-is-connection-quality-determined)). Paeonia uses loss, round trip and the room the network leaves the video, each with a bad and a good level and a streak in between. The same detector library keeps an encoder that is short of CPU apart from the network ([its README](https://github.com/VLprojects/webrtc-issue-detector)), so `qualityLimitationReason` `cpu` never counts as a weak connection.
- **A freeze threshold meant for counting.** WebRTC's stats count a freeze from 3 times the average gap between the last 30 frames, or the average plus 150 ms ([W3C webrtc-stats, `freezeCount`](https://www.w3.org/TR/webrtc-stats/#dom-rtcinboundrtpstreamstats-freezecount)). That is right for a counter, but on screen it would blur every short hitch. Detectors that warn about frozen video wait for freezes of more than a second on average ([webrtc-issue-detector](https://github.com/VLprojects/webrtc-issue-detector/blob/master/src/detectors/FrozenVideoTrackDetector.ts)), so Paeonia blurs from 1 second.
- **A camera that stops on an iPhone for a reason meant for iPads.** Developers have seen `videoDeviceNotAvailableWithMultipleForegroundApps` on iPhones without finding why ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/769996)). Paeonia treats every interruption reason as a pause, so an unexpected one still shows the partner a pause and not a frozen frame.
- **Ignoring heat on long video calls.** At `serious` iOS lowers the frame rate of FaceTime, and at `critical` Apple asks apps to stop using the camera ([WWDC19 session 422](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2019/422/)). Couples call for hours at 1080p, so the video steps down with the heat and the call offers to turn the camera off at `critical`.
- **The side button.** A side button that only locks the screen during a call came from the app's audio handling and route, not the system. Test the side button with the call on the receiver. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/852594))
- **A broadcast extension for screen sharing.** Before iOS 27 the whole screen could only be shared from a ReplayKit Broadcast Upload Extension, a separate process with a 50 MB memory limit. On iPads ReplayKit held up to 6 full-size frames at once while the phone was busy, and extensions crashed at the limit. Every team also had to carry frames from the extension to the app over a socket. Paeonia captures with ScreenCaptureKit inside the app instead. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/651367), [LiveKit's iOS screen sharing guide](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/blob/main/Docs/ios-screen-sharing.md))
- **A broadcast picker with a look of its own.** `RPSystemBroadcastPickerView` starts a broadcast only from a tap on its own button, which has a fixed look. Apple DTS called changing its icon through the picker's subviews SPI and a cause for App Review rejection ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/112978)). Teams that start it from their own button send an action to that inner button ([LiveKit's `BroadcastManager`](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/blob/main/Sources/LiveKit/Broadcast/BroadcastManager.swift)) or hide the picker and show it from native code ([Jitsi's `ScreenSharingIosButton`](https://github.com/jitsi/jitsi-meet/blob/master/react/features/toolbox/components/native/ScreenSharingIosButton.tsx)), which breaks when Apple changes the view. iOS 27 deprecates the picker for ScreenCaptureKit's `SCContentSharingPicker` (`RPBroadcast.h`), so Share is a plain button that asks the engine to show the system's picker.
- **A still screen sends nothing.** ScreenCaptureKit delivers a frame only when the screen changes. The encoder repairs a lost frame only with a new one, so on a still screen the partner's picture could stay broken. LiveKit sends the last frame again every second, and so does `CallScreenCapture`. ([LiveKit pull request 1135](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/pull/1135))
- **A share stopped from the system's indicator.** LiveKit's iOS share stayed published, frozen on the last frame, after the person stopped it from the system's indicator, because only its own stop path cleaned up. In Paeonia the stream's `didStopWithError` runs the same stop as the share button. ([LiveKit pull request 1135](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/pull/1135))
- **The `screen-capture` background mode.** Without it iOS stops the stream with error -3824 as soon as the app leaves the screen, and keeping the app running with audio does not help. Until August 2026 App Store Connect refused uploads that declared it, until Apple fixed its list of allowed values. If an upload fails with "UIBackgroundModes contains an invalid value: 'screen-capture'", file feedback. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/840821))
- **An entitlement that does not exist.** Apple's iOS sample asks for `com.apple.developer.screen-recording`, and with it provisioning fails. Apple DTS says ScreenCaptureKit needs no entitlement on iOS. ([Apple forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/856067))
- **One picker for the whole app.** `SCContentSharingPicker.shared` serves the whole app, and only while it is marked active does it show or take a share from system UI. Paeonia marks it active only while it is up, as LiveKit does. ([LiveKit's `IOSScreenCapturer`](https://github.com/livekit/client-sdk-swift/blob/main/Sources/LiveKit/Track/Capturers/IOSScreenCapturer.swift))
- **WebRTC's screen-sharing mode on an iPhone.** `videoSource(forScreenCast: true)` puts LiveKit's H.265 and H.264 encoders in screen-sharing mode, which sets VideoToolbox's `MaxAllowedFrameQP` to 39. Apple says an encoder that reaches that cap and runs out of bits drops frames to keep the bitrate. WebRTC's own frame dropper drops more, because iOS encoders do not report a rate controller it trusts, and the mode turns off the quality scaler that would send a smaller picture instead. Paeonia's first share reached the partner at 1 to 5 frames a second between two iPhone 18 Pro Max phones, and `scripts/call-media-probe` measures 10 frames a second direct and 4 on TURN in that mode. Paeonia's screen goes through a plain video source (`CallCodecs.screenSource`). ([RTCVideoEncoderH265.mm](https://github.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/blob/m150_release/sdk/objc/components/video_codec/RTCVideoEncoderH265.mm), [WWDC21 session 10158](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10158/))
- **A shared screen that keeps its resolution.** `maintainResolution`, WebRTC's choice for a screen, answers a busy encoder or a short network only with fewer frames, two thirds of the rate at each step, down to 2 frames a second. Text stays sharp in a picture that barely moves. Paeonia sends the screen with `maintainFramerate`, so WebRTC lowers the size first. ([video_stream_adapter.cc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/webrtc-sdk/webrtc/83af826464a3212bf452fcb3a8d0619e7d77a368/call/adaptation/video_stream_adapter.cc))

## Testing

The simulator has no system call UI, so iOS ends every LiveCommunicationKit call there at once. Simulator builds use `SimulatorCallSystem` instead. Two simulators on a local backend cover starting, answering, declining and ending a call. Simulators get no VoIP pushes, so the developer tests the rest on two real phones: ringing a locked phone, answering from the Lock Screen, a closed app, AirPods and car audio, a network change mid-call, and the side button. Connecting while the call rings needs two phones too: the time from the answer to the connection in the `CallStats` log, the caller's ringback staying as loud as before while the partner's phone rings, no microphone indicator on a ringing phone, and a partner with an iPhone and an iPad that both ring, answered on each. Two audio cases were only simulated, by posting the system's notifications inside the app, and still need a phone: an interruption during a call, such as a timer that goes off, and a restart of the audio service (Settings, Developer, Reset Media Services).

Screen sharing needs two phones too, because ScreenCaptureKit shares only on a device. `scripts/call-media-probe` checks the third media section, the screen's frame rate and size, and the camera's share of the budget on each path and on a warm and a hot phone, on two connections in one Mac process. On the phones, check that the share button shows the system's picker and sharing starts after picking, that the partner sees the screen within a second and keeps seeing it after the sharer opens another app and locks and unlocks, that a still screen stays sharp, that scrolling and a playing video reach the partner smoothly, near 30 frames a second on the stats line, with the sharing phone no warmer than in FaceTime's share, that the sharer's camera keeps its shape in both viewfinders when a share starts and ends, and comes back at full size after a share ended from the system's indicator in another app, that the share follows the sharer turning their phone, that stopping from the share button, from the system's indicator and by ending the call all stop it on both phones, that Picture in Picture shows the partner's screen while they share and their camera after, and the call's stats line (`CallStats` in Console) while both the camera and the screen send, on Wi-Fi and with location sharing paused so the call goes through TURN.

## Open questions

The review on 2026-11-15 found the next three. Each needs a test on a phone, or a decision, before any code changes.

- Answering a ring inside the app while a Focus silences calls. `answer()` reports the call to the system first, and iOS refuses to ring for a silenced call, so the answer fails and the caller sees no answer. Test it with Do Not Disturb on. If it fails, the call could run without the system call UI, as it does on the simulator, or start as an outgoing conversation, which a Focus does not filter.
- A call declined on the person's other device. That device's row goes away like a cancelled call, so the phone ends its ring as missed and Recents shows a missed call. LiveCommunicationKit has a `declinedElsewhere` reason, which needs the server to say why the row went away.
- The partner's name on a ring that arrives on a locked phone comes from a cache that the app writes when it opens and when a call ends. After a nickname change it shows the old name until then.

- Whether the partner hears game sounds during a call. They play in the call's engine, which the echo canceller hears. Play Ludo on the speaker during a call between two phones and listen on the other phone.
- Whether score, call, chat and "•••" fit in every game toolbar on the smallest supported iPhone. Check each game with `scripts/scenario-screenshot` once the button exists.
- Whether notification banners show in a shared screen. If they do, the partner can read the sharer's messages from other people while they share. FaceTime's own screen sharing is the comparison.
- Whether ScreenCaptureKit marks a turned phone's frames with `SCStreamFrameInfo.videoOrientation`, as ReplayKit did, or turns them itself. `CallScreenCapture` follows the mark when it is there.
